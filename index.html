About Adimi

Redefining how AI supports creative learning

Art education is not only about producing a better-looking picture. It is about learning to observe, experiment, make choices, understand outcomes, and develop your own way of thinking.

Yet most digital learning tools focus on delivering instructions, while generative AI can go even further by creating the artwork itself. Adimi takes a different approach — we build technology that supports the creative process without taking ownership of it.

Through short art lessons, guided intention-setting, offline drawing, AI-assisted observation and learner reflection, Adimi helps students turn their own artwork into an opportunity to learn.

Our Positioning

AI should support creativity, not replace it.

Why Adimi

Built around learner agency

Student first, AI second

Before seeing the AI's observation, students first describe what they see in their own artwork. This makes AI feedback something to compare with, rather than something to automatically follow.

No automatic judgment

Adimi does not tell students whether their artwork is good or bad. A result that differs from the original intention is not automatically treated as an error.

Measurable observation

Our MVP begins with deterministic computer vision rather than asking a language model to freely interpret an image. The system measures visual information through image processing and a 3×3 grid, then either describes the result or explicitly refuses to make a conclusion when the data is insufficient.

Designed with boundaries

Adimi does not draw for students, grade their artwork, evaluate talent, compare students, or infer personality or psychological states.

Our Solution

A complete learning loop — from intention to reflection

Adimi turns one drawing session into a structured learning experience.

01 · Learn

A short art lesson introduces one concept.

02 · Set an Intention

Students choose references, answer guiding questions and define what they want to explore.

03 · Draw

Students draw independently on paper. AI steps away from the creative process.

04 · Observe

Students photograph their work and describe what they see before receiving AI feedback.

05 · Decide

Students choose: Keep · Edit · Think Further.

06 · Reflect

Students reflect on the decision they have made.

One concept. One learning loop. Four sessions.

MVP & Impact

From a blank page to a meaningful decision

Our MVP explores a simple question: can AI provide useful information without becoming the authority in a learner's creative process?

In one example session, a student sets an intention around line density, creates a pencil drawing on A4 paper, observes the result independently, and then receives one AI-generated description based on measurable visual data. The AI does not tell the student what to fix. Instead, it asks the student to decide whether the difference is an intention, a discovery, or something to change.

What We Are Learning

We measure more than whether the app works.

Adimi's MVP is designed to test whether learners can actually use feedback to make their own decisions. Our pilot focuses on three questions:

Feasibility

Can students complete the full learning loop?

Agency

Can students choose to keep, edit or think further, and explain their decision in their own words?

Feedback boundaries

Can the system provide useful observations without turning feedback into grading, commands or unsupported interpretation?

Our Development Journey

From one concept to a scalable learning experience

Stage 01

Problem Discovery

Understanding how students respond to feedback and whether AI observation changes their decision-making.

Stage 02

Measurement Validation

Testing whether the visual measurement system remains stable across different drawing scenarios and common mobile devices.

Stage 03

Thin MVP

Building and piloting the complete learning loop with students learning independently at home.

Our next milestone: will students come back for the second session?

Because for Adimi, a successful learning product is not simply one that works once. It is one that makes learners want to observe, decide and learn again.

Our Values

Human-centred by design

Agency

We believe learners should remain the authors of their own decisions.

Curiosity

Unexpected results are not necessarily mistakes. They can become discoveries.

Evidence

AI should distinguish between what it can measure and what it cannot know.

Responsibility

Technology for children needs clear boundaries — especially when AI is involved.

Creativity

The goal is not to make AI more creative than the learner. The goal is to help the learner become more creative.

Trust & Child Safety

Technology children can learn with, and adults can trust.

Adimi is intentionally designed with boundaries around what the system can and cannot do. We do not use children's artwork to train models, do not grade their artistic ability, and do not infer personality or psychological states from their work.

Our architecture is also designed toward on-device image processing, reducing the need for children's artwork to leave the device.

Child safety is not an additional feature. It is part of the product architecture.
